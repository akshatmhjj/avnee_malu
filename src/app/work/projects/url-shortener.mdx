## Overview

This project evaluates Eli Lilly and Co. as a global pharmaceutical leader, with particular attention to the growth and concentration of its GLP-1 franchise. The report combines company analysis, operating assumptions, a discounted cash flow valuation, financing review, M&A discussion, and scenario analysis.

## Investment thesis

- FY2025 revenue of $65.2B, with an 83% gross margin and approximately 56% of sales from GLP-1 drugs.
- Mounjaro and Zepbound generated approximately $36.5B in FY2025 revenue and remain the primary near-term growth engine.
- Orforglipron, an oral GLP-1 candidate, could expand the addressable market by reaching patients who are unwilling or unable to use injectables.
- Oncology and other pipeline investments are intended to diversify Lilly beyond obesity and diabetes therapies.

## Valuation framework

- Product demand currently exceeds supply, supporting pricing power and strong operating margins.
- Revenue growth is expected to normalize from 25.0% in 2026 to 7.0% in 2030 as the market matures.
- Core EBIT margin is forecast to remain resilient, moving from 46.5% in 2026 to 44.5% in 2030.
- The central risk is that long-term GLP-1 adoption reaches only a fraction of the eligible patient population, leaving current market expectations too optimistic.

The model uses a 6.9% WACC and a 2.8% terminal growth rate, reflecting Lilly's defensive pharmaceutical profile, cash flow visibility, and relatively low reliance on debt.

- Enterprise value: $816.7B
- Equity value: $784.3B
- Implied share price: $877.32
- Reference market price: $955.19
- Implied downside: 8.2%

The base DCF indicates that Lilly is modestly overvalued even after incorporating strong near-term growth and durable margins.

## Risks and scenarios

- Medicare coverage uncertainty, pricing regulation, MFN policy, and potential tariffs could reduce realized revenue or compress margins.
- Competition from Novo Nordisk, Pfizer, Amgen, and other GLP-1 developers could erode market share and pricing power.
- Debt increased from $25B in 2023 to $42.5B in 2025, but leverage improved to approximately 1.6x D/E and interest coverage remains near 30x.
- High out-of-pocket costs and competition from compounding pharmacies could limit patient access and volume growth.

Lilly is unlikely to be acquired because of its approximately $900B market capitalization, roughly 40x P/E, and the regulatory barriers to a large pharmaceutical merger. As a strategic acquirer, however, Lilly has the financial capacity to pursue pipeline assets and replace future patent losses.

- Bull case: $1,133.95, supported by a successful Foundayo launch, revenue above $80B, and voluntary MFN pricing.
- Base case: $1,000-$1,100, supported by a dominant U.S. position, global expansion, and steady earnings growth.
- Bear case: $650-$700, driven by mandatory MFN pricing, tariffs, margin pressure, and stronger competition.

## Project impact

Lilly has an unusually strong growth and profitability profile, but the valuation leaves limited room for execution misses. The investment case depends on sustained GLP-1 adoption, continued manufacturing scale-up, and successful diversification of the pipeline while regulatory and competitive pressures remain manageable.
